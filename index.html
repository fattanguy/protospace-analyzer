    How the frame is read

    The frame is looked at from the front. Every metre of the face is classed by how deep its first cube sits: black at the front, grey a few metres back, light grey deep inside, white where you see straight through. Modules are 9 m wide and 3 m tall; the module grid is placed where the full/empty reading is clearest. 1 cube = 1 m.

    Scores describe fit to this project's targets, not architectural quality. They are a tool for comparing frames, not a verdict.

    How bays are rated

    A frame has five bays: equal parts across the length of the slab (25 or 24 m each on the 123 m slab), the full height and the full depth — as square as the slab allows. Each bay gets the twelve descriptor ratings from its own depth reading (the same analyzer as the Analyze tab), and a handful of counts straight from its cubes: solid cubes, floors (empty cubes standing on mass with 3 m of headroom), sheltered floors (mass somewhere overhead), a roofed hall at the ground, open ground, see-through columns, and the pockets, streets and openings of its face.

    Each use is a 0–100 score: 60 % from the descriptors that matter for it, 40 % from the counts. Lobby wants Porous, Visually Connected, Networked, Socially Interactive and Layered, plus a roofed hall at the ground, open ground and an open (not stuffed) bay. Gathering wants Socially Interactive, Decentralized, Clustered, Village-Like, Intimate and Layered, plus sheltered floor, gathering pockets and floor with headroom. Working wants Modular, Intimate, Clustered, Layered and Interlocking, plus floor with headroom, enough mass to make rooms (30–60 % solid) and enclosure (few see-through columns).

    The red in the model marks exactly what a reading counts — every cube of mass, all the empty space, the enclosed voids with mass on three or more sides, the floors, the sheltered floors, the roofed hall, the open ground, the see-through columns, or the pockets, streets and openings of the face — so you can check the count against what you see. The scores describe fit to these simple rules, not whether a design is good; use them to compare bays and frames.
